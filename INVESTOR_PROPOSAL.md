# LagosGo — Investor Proposal
**Confidential discussion draft | October 2026**  
**Investment target: ₦350 million | 18-month Lagos-first launch**

## 1. The proposition

LagosGo is building a driver-first mobility network for Lagos, designed to expand across Nigeria. We aim to make everyday rides more dependable through professional drivers, clean and roadworthy vehicles, transparent fares, accurate pickup estimates, live trip visibility, and accountable support.

The business will launch supply-first: recruit and verify professional drivers and fleet partners, secure recurring corporate ride demand, and open passenger bookings only after the core trip journey works end to end.

**Investment thesis:** build a trusted operating network—not merely an app download count.

## 2. The customer problem

- **Passengers:** uncertain pickup timing, inconsistent vehicle quality, and limited visibility when a journey is underway.
- **Drivers:** opaque fee structures, uneven access to profitable requests, and the need for clear payout and dispute records.
- **Companies:** staff commuting, shift transport, airport trips and visitor journeys often require dependable scheduling, central oversight and consolidated reporting.
- **Cities:** fragmented transport choices create friction for people and businesses.

## 3. The LagosGo solution

### Passenger experience
1. Enter pickup and destination or use permission-based current location.
2. View a traffic-aware road route, trip-duration estimate and distance when mapping services are configured.
3. After a real driver is matched, see driver/vehicle details and a pickup ETA calculated from that driver's live position and road route.
4. During a live ride, observe vehicle position and route progress, see turn guidance and useful verified landmarks, and optionally share the trip.
5. Receive a receipt, rate the trip and access a documented support process.

**Important readiness distinction:** the current web app is a prototype. Route-preview code and landmark lookup are present, but production API configuration must be verified. Live driver matching, pickup ETA, live GPS tracking, payments, authentication and emergency response are not yet production-ready. We will not describe these as live until they are built and tested end to end.

### Driver experience
- Clear fare breakdown and visible platform fee.
- Driver earnings records and a transparent payout ledger.
- Fair matching based on road ETA, eligibility, reliability and earnings fairness.
- Flexible participation, clear service rules, support and an auditable complaints process.
- Vehicle inspection and documented standards before activation.

### Corporate mobility
Offer staff rides, shift transport, airport transfers, executive mobility, visitor trips and scheduled routes. The planned corporate account experience includes authorised bookers, ride policies, trip reporting, cost centres and consolidated invoices. Corporate pricing and service levels will be agreed contractually before activation.

## 4. Driver quality is the brand

All participating drivers and vehicles must meet the applicable legal and operational requirements. LagosGo's proposed service standard includes:

- Clean interior and exterior, tidy seats/windows/boot and no persistent unpleasant odours.
- Working air conditioning for eligible vehicle categories, with any approved exception disclosed before booking.
- Roadworthy condition, seat belts, lights, tyres and valid vehicle/driver documents.
- Appropriate insurance, respectful conduct, safe driving and no smoking during trips.
- Inspection at onboarding, periodic checks, rider feedback and a documented corrective-action process.

Specific legal, insurance, vehicle-age and driver eligibility requirements must be validated with Nigerian advisers before the pilot.

## 5. Go-to-market: supply first, contracts second, public demand third

### Phase A — build supply (months 0–3)
- Recruit independent drivers and fleet operators in selected Lagos zones.
- Inspect vehicles, verify documents, train drivers and publish fee/payout terms.
- Target 150 verified pilot drivers, subject to service capacity.
- Establish support coverage, safety escalation and a driver advisory group.

### Phase B — secure recurring demand (months 1–6)
- Approach employers, professional-services firms, hotels, hospitals, event organisers and airport-facing businesses.
- Secure five anchor corporate pilot partners.
- Agree operating zones, schedules, vehicle classes, service windows, billing and reporting.
- Use contract demand to improve utilisation before large consumer-advertising spend.

### Phase C — controlled passenger pilot (months 4–9)
- Invite riders into a limited service area.
- Test booking, driver offer/acceptance, pickup ETA, live trip updates, completion, receipts, cancellations, payment reconciliation and support.
- Suggested gate: 2,500 completed rides per month, at least 90% completion after driver acceptance, and no unresolved critical safety/payment defects before expanding. These are proposed launch targets, not current performance.

### Phase D — scale Lagos (months 7–18)
Expand zone by zone based on demand, pickup reliability, driver earnings, corporate retention and unit economics. Expand to another state only after Lagos meets agreed quality and cash-runway thresholds.

## 6. Product and download strategy

- **Now:** mobile-first website and PWA pilot shell, installable from the website on supported devices. Android users can use the browser's Install option; iPhone users use Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen.
- **Before public bookings:** production authentication, driver app workflow, durable ride state, matching/atomic assignment, live GPS updates, payment/payout ledger, support tooling, privacy/security review and end-to-end tests.
- **Before app stores:** device QA, app-store privacy disclosures, native packaging decision, store assets, test cohort feedback and store review.
- The PWA shell can cache the interface; it does not make live booking, dispatch or payments work offline.

## 7. Business model

1. **Primary revenue:** a transparent service fee/commission on completed rides.
2. **Corporate programmes:** agreed account-management or programme fees where they create clear value.
3. **Later expansion:** scheduled airport products, fleet tools and parcel/errand delivery after the core ride service is reliable.

The base case below uses an **illustrative 10% effective platform take rate**. This is a modelling assumption, not an approved public tariff. The final fee and cap must be tested with drivers and passengers and approved before launch.

## 8. Illustrative three-year financial model

These figures are planning scenarios, not audited forecasts or guaranteed outcomes. Gross bookings (GMV) are the total fares paid for rides; platform revenue is the assumed service fee only. The model excludes additional food/delivery revenue, corporate account fees and other unbuilt services.

| Metric | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Completed rides | 240,000 | 1,200,000 | 3,600,000 |
| Average fare | ₦4,000 | ₦4,500 | ₦5,000 |
| Gross bookings (GMV) | ₦960m | ₦5.40bn | ₦18.0bn |
| Platform revenue at 10% | ₦96m | ₦540m | ₦1.80bn |
| Illustrative operating costs | ₦300m | ₦480m | ₦1.10bn |
| Operating surplus/(deficit) | (₦204m) | ₦60m | ₦700m |

**Volume sanity check:** 3.6 million annual rides equals about 300,000 rides per month at the Year 3 run-rate. That could require approximately 4,000 active drivers averaging three completed rides per day over 26 days per month. Driver productivity, demand, fares and zone density must be validated in the pilot.

The operating surplus/(deficit) shown is before tax, financing, depreciation, expansion capital expenditure and any unmodelled costs. This model must be rebuilt from actual cohort data after the pilot.

## 9. Capital request — ₦350 million

The target is to finance approximately 18 months of disciplined execution. This is an asset-light platform plan; it does **not** include purchasing a vehicle fleet.

| Use of funds | Amount | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Product/backend, maps, security and QA | ₦80m | 23% |
| Driver recruitment, onboarding, inspections and pilot support | ₦65m | 19% |
| Corporate sales and fleet partnerships | ₦40m | 11% |
| Customer acquisition and launch marketing | ₦35m | 10% |
| Operations, support, safety, compliance and insurance | ₦55m | 16% |
| Working capital and contingency | ₦75m | 21% |
| **Total** | **₦350m** | **100%** |

Funding should be released against milestones with monthly reporting, cash controls and an explicit contingency reserve.

## 10. Investor return framework

The financing instrument, valuation and investor protections should be negotiated after diligence. Possible structures include equity, a convertible instrument or milestone-based financing.

**Illustration only:** if an investor contributed ₦350m for 20% fully diluted ownership, a later ₦3.5bn equity valuation would imply a paper stake of ₦700m (2.0× gross). A ₦5.25bn equity valuation would imply ₦1.05bn (3.0× gross). These calculations are not a valuation forecast, offer or guarantee; they exclude dilution, tax, debt, transaction costs and illiquidity. The investor could lose some or all of the capital.

Dividends should only be considered after positive cash flow, reserves, obligations and reinvestment requirements are covered. The board should receive monthly reporting on completed rides, contribution margin, driver retention, corporate recurring revenue, cancellations, safety incidents and cash runway.

## 11. Expansion: one company, local pride

LagosGo will be the flagship. Potential future state/city brands include **AbujaGo, IbadanGo, EnuguGo and PHGo**, subject to name/trademark clearance and local research.

Each market should have local driver recruitment, corporate sales, community relationships and customer support. The technology, safety standards, payments ledger, analytics and release process remain shared. State expansion is earned by performance, not by a launch announcement.

## 12. Risks and controls

- **Driver supply without enough trips:** recruit by zone and build corporate demand before mass marketing.
- **Unreliable pickup estimates:** calculate pickup ETA from an actual matched driver's live position and road routing; never confuse it with trip duration.
- **Vehicle-quality or safety incidents:** verified onboarding, inspections, support escalation, documented incident handling and insurance.
- **Cash burn:** staged investment, capped incentives, unit-economics reporting and an asset-light model.
- **Regulatory, tax, insurance and privacy duties:** obtain qualified Nigerian advice before public launch.
- **Competitive pressure:** compete on service consistency, driver economics and corporate accountability rather than unsustainable discounts.

## 13. The ask

LagosGo is seeking **₦350 million in seed capital** and strategic partnerships to execute an 18-month Lagos-first plan.

Priority partners:
- Professional driver networks and fleet operators.
- Corporate transport buyers and anchor employers.
- Payment and mapping providers.
- Insurance, safety, legal and data-protection advisers.

The immediate work is to validate the model, configure production services, complete the backend, sign initial corporate pilots, recruit the first driver cohort and test real rides. LagosGo will only open public bookings after the core journey works reliably from request to pickup, trip completion and payment reconciliation.

**LagosGo — Move Lagos. Move Nigeria.**
