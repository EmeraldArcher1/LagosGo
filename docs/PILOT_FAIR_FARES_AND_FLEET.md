# LagosGo Pilot: Fair Fares, Driver Economics, Fleet Standards and Public-Interest Case

**Status: proposal for product, legal, operations and investor review. Not a live policy or implemented feature.**

## Purpose

LagosGo should make trips safer and more predictable while allowing drivers, vehicle owners, the platform and investors to sustain the service. The Lagos pilot should be locally grounded, measurable and designed to retain value in Nigeria. It must not promise a fare, driver income, discount, return or public-sector partnership before the underlying model and approvals are validated.

## 1. Fair fares in Lagos traffic

Do not use a conventional distance-only meter that keeps increasing without a clear rule while a vehicle is trapped in traffic. Before a passenger confirms a ride, show a fare estimate and explain the components. A recommended fare model to test is:

- A disclosed base/pickup component.
- A route-distance component based on a routing provider's road route.
- A disclosed time component with a **traffic/waiting cap or protection rule**.
- Clearly disclosed tolls, airport/parking charges or other approved pass-through costs.
- Applicable taxes and the platform fee treatment, shown transparently.
- A bounded adjustment process for passenger-requested stops, destination changes or exceptional route changes.

### Traffic-protection experiment

Test a pre-quoted fare for ordinary trips, with a reasonable tolerance band rather than an endlessly running meter. If actual traffic makes the trip substantially longer, use a pre-disclosed rule: for example, cap the traffic-time component after a defined threshold, or absorb part of extraordinary delay through a company-funded traffic-protection pool. Never silently deduct the protection amount from the driver's agreed earnings.

The exact threshold, cap and pool must be set only after pilot data and unit-economics modelling. Drivers must be able to see the minimum guaranteed payout before accepting. If a fare must change for a material reason, request passenger confirmation where practical and log the reason. A passenger must not be charged twice for the same component.

### Cost model

Model each vehicle/service category and time band using:
- Fuel or energy price and consumption, including petrol, diesel, CNG and EV charging where relevant.
- Trip distance, expected traffic and idle time, pickup distance and dead mileage.
- Maintenance, tyres, repairs, depreciation/lease or finance costs, insurance, licensing and inspection.
- Driver take-home pay, rest time, mobile data and reasonable household obligations as part of driver research—not as a basis for collecting unnecessary sensitive family data.
- Payment processing, support, safety operations, refunds, fraud losses, maps/cloud/communications, office and staff costs.
- Company-owned fleet costs and reserves, platform revenue, tax and investor return requirements.

Report contribution margin per trip, driver net earnings per online hour, passenger price, cancellation rates, traffic exposure, and platform/fleet costs. Run downside scenarios for fuel-price increases, low demand, high traffic, vehicle downtime and promotions. Do not subsidise permanently loss-making trips without a budgeted funding source.

## 2. Driver-first economics

- Show the driver the passenger fare, platform fee, any payment-processing deduction, incentive and expected payout before acceptance.
- Use a published, capped platform fee schedule; never change the rate retroactively.
- Maintain an append-only ledger for fare, fees, incentives, refunds, tips and payout adjustments.
- Reconcile payouts and provide a clear dispute path and transaction history.
- Evaluate earnings per online hour and net earnings after realistic operating costs, not gross trip receipts alone.
- Offer opt-in, budget-capped incentives funded by LagosGo, fleet partners, merchants or sponsors. Promotions must not be financed by undisclosed reductions in driver pay.
- Give drivers a documented appeal and human review for safety or account restrictions. Avoid automatic deactivation based solely on a single low rating or opaque score.
- Provide driver support, rest and fatigue guidance, and clear incident escalation.

## 3. Vehicle-owner and fleet-partner programme

Allow individuals, SMEs and fleet operators to register eligible cars. Owners may drive themselves or employ a driver under their own lawful agreement; LagosGo should not dictate a private employment agreement, but must set minimum safety, service and payment-transparency requirements.

### Proposed vehicle eligibility checklist

- Valid registration, roadworthiness inspection and required insurance/licences.
- Reliable engine/drivetrain, safe tyres, working seat belts, lights and brakes.
- Functional air conditioning for categories where promised.
- Clean, odour-free, well-maintained interior and exterior.
- Vehicle age and condition limits set after safety, reliability and affordability analysis.
- Verified vehicle owner and driver identity, lawful authority to use the vehicle, and periodic document renewal.
- Passenger capacity and luggage suitability accurately declared.
- Safety inspection and photo evidence; periodic re-inspection and complaint-triggered checks.

Define separate service categories if older but safe vehicles can serve a lower-cost category. Do not relax safety-critical requirements to increase supply.

### Brand identification without compulsory repainting

Keep the iconic yellow-and-black Lagos taxi influence in the brand system, while allowing eligible private vehicles to retain their original paint during the pilot. Test a controlled identity package:
- Tamper-evident, numbered windshield/side decals or removable magnetic panels.
- A unique, time-bound driver/vehicle verification QR or code displayed only in the app and on approved identifiers.
- Driver photo, vehicle make/model, registration details and trip PIN shown to the passenger before pickup.
- A secure, platform-issued roof sign or removable badge only if approved by relevant transport authorities and designed not to obstruct visibility or violate vehicle rules.
- A clear method for reporting an identifier that does not match the app.

A cone is not suitable as a moving-vehicle identifier. No decal, badge, QR or roof sign alone should be treated as proof of authenticity; the passenger should match the in-app vehicle and driver details. Have legal/transport authorities review placement, colour, signage and any licensing implications before rollout.

## 4. Community-focused Lagos pilot

Position the pilot as a Lagos-based opportunity to improve local mobility, create accountable driver/owner participation and retain more economic value locally. Publish objective, auditable eligibility criteria for pilot recruitment, service areas, vehicle classes and incentives.

The initial pilot may prioritise people who live and work in Lagos and Lagos-based vehicle owners, alongside other eligible participants under the published rules. Any indigene-only, ancestry-based or state-of-origin restriction requires Nigerian legal and public-policy review before it is advertised or enforced. Do not infer or collect ethnic identity. If a public programme is intended to benefit Lagos indigenes specifically, seek a lawful, transparent design with government counsel and consider residency, local business participation or other objective criteria.

Expansion to another state should be a new, locally consulted pilot with published eligibility and local operating requirements—not an automatic assumption that state-of-origin status is the only fair criterion.

## 5. Safety and competitor-informed capability checklist

Competitor features are research inputs, not instructions to copy a competitor's brand or proprietary design. Before launch, evaluate each capability for user need, legal basis, cost, reliability and measurable benefit.

### Ride basics
- Passenger and driver authentication, recovery and role-based access.
- Driver/vehicle verification, document expiry alerts and approval history.
- Pickup pin plus editable landmark notes, destination confirmation and road-based ETA.
- Upfront itemised estimate, ride categories, scheduled trips where operationally supported.
- Driver offer/acceptance with atomic assignment, no double-booking and clear timeout/retry.
- Live trip states, route progress and trusted trip sharing.
- Cancellation reasons, clear cancellation charges, refunds and dispute handling.
- Receipts, trip history, payment method status and support access.

### Safety and trust
- Trip PIN/OTP at pickup, vehicle/driver match, voluntary trusted-contact sharing and safety check-ins.
- One-tap access to locally verified emergency guidance and an incident-reporting route.
- Masked calling/messaging where supported; privacy-aware retention and access logs.
- Mutual post-trip feedback, structured issue reporting, appeals and human review.
- Fraud detection for account takeover, GPS spoofing, duplicate incentives, fake trips and payment abuse.
- Safety operations staffed and tested before claims about 24/7 support or emergency response are made.

### Driver tools
- Clear trip payout and deductions before acceptance.
- Earnings dashboard, payout status, receipts, incentive progress and online-hour metrics.
- Availability controls, navigation hand-off, issue reporting and vehicle-document renewal.
- Fair dispatch, stale-location checks, duplicate offer prevention and reason-coded rejection/cancellation analytics.
- Fleet manager roles, vehicle assignment, compliance reminders and reconciled fleet reporting.

### Passenger convenience and future services
- Book for another passenger with clear passenger/payer consent.
- Pay-for-me and wallet gifting only after secure payments, ledgers, consent, limits and reversal handling are implemented.
- Airport transfers with verified pickup instructions and disclosed waiting rules.
- Parcel/errand delivery with restricted-item policy, chain-of-custody events, recipient OTP and proof of delivery.
- Corporate accounts, authorised bookers, cost centres, receipts and support SLAs.
- Loyalty, referrals, off-peak rewards and shared rides only with budget, abuse controls and validated operations.
- Local discovery and merchant services only when listings, availability, payment, refunds and support are real and maintained.

## 6. Technical, security and launch gates

- Preserve the existing GitHub main branch and working Vercel deployment; develop in feature branches and review changes through pull requests.
- Do not assume the Supabase migration has been applied. Verify the correct project and schema read-only before any migration; never connect or modify an unrelated project.
- Keep service-role keys and provider secrets server-side. Restrict public keys, validate all inputs server-side and enforce row-level security.
- Use server-authoritative pricing, state transitions and ledger writes; idempotency for bookings, payments, refunds and rewards.
- Add unit, integration, end-to-end, abuse, accessibility and failure-path tests; monitor logs and error rates.
- Do not advertise live dispatch, tracking, payments, push alerts, verified drivers, insurance or emergency response until each is implemented and tested.
- Complete legal, insurance, tax, data-protection, transport licensing and consumer-protection reviews before public launch.

## 7. Public-sector and investor proposition

LagosGo seeks to become a responsible local mobility operator and technology partner—not simply a commission collector. Potential public value includes more accountable mobility supply, vehicle standards, better passenger information, owner-driver participation, local jobs, auditable service performance and evidence to guide transport planning.

Potential engagement with Lagos State Government, the relevant transport agencies and Transport for Lagos should be framed as a request for consultation, a controlled pilot and measurable evaluation. Do not imply endorsement or partnership before formal approval.

A pilot proposal should define geography, duration, eligible cohorts, safety controls, fleet mix, fare experiment, public reporting, complaint escalation, data-sharing limits, evaluation metrics, exit criteria and who pays for incentives. Government access to identifiable trip data should not be presumed.

### Investor model and reporting

Investors should receive a transparent use-of-funds plan and conservative/base/upside scenarios. Separate platform economics from any company-owned fleet economics. Show:
- Revenue by platform fee, fleet services and approved partner services.
- Driver net earnings and payout timeliness.
- Contribution margin per ride and break-even trips/day.
- Fleet utilisation, maintenance reserve, downtime and depreciation if vehicles are owned.
- Passenger acquisition/retention, match time, cancellations and complaint resolution.
- Safety incidents, verified driver/vehicle rate, refund/fraud loss and privacy/security events.
- Incentive spend by funder and remaining budget.
- Capital required, runway, dilution/repayment terms and investor return assumptions with explicit risks.

Never promise guaranteed returns, guaranteed income, or government procurement. Any projection must be labelled as a forecast and tied to assumptions.

## 8. Pilot scorecard and decision gates

Set baselines before public pilot and review weekly:
- Median and 90th-percentile driver pickup ETA.
- Passenger price and fare variance versus estimate.
- Driver net earnings per online hour after estimated operating costs.
- Platform contribution margin per completed ride.
- Driver and passenger cancellation rates, completion rate and support resolution time.
- Safety incidents per trip, verification coverage and unresolved high-severity cases.
- Vehicle inspection pass rate, downtime and complaints.
- Geographic and time-of-day coverage, accessibility feedback and repeat use.
- Fraud/refund loss, incentive efficiency and privacy/security incidents.

Expand only if safety, driver economics, passenger fairness, operational reliability and unit economics meet pre-agreed thresholds. Pause a zone or feature when safety controls or financial sustainability fail.

## 9. Government policy request: public-transport priority corridors

**Proposal for consultation; not an existing entitlement, approved scheme or confirmed government partnership.**

LagosGo proposes that Lagos State assess a controlled public-transport priority corridor pilot to improve journey reliability for passengers and drivers during congestion. The policy should be designed for the wider public transport network, not solely for LagosGo.

### Existing BRT rules and the request

The proposal must recognise the existing BRT Lite corridor regulations, which restrict use of BRT lanes to franchised operators for whose services the lanes were built, with specified emergency-service exceptions. LagosGo, private taxis and other e-hailing vehicles must not enter a restricted BRT lane unless expressly authorised under the applicable rules.

We request that the relevant authorities—including the Ministry of Transportation, LAMATA, LASTMA and other responsible road/traffic agencies—consider whether a separate or adapted corridor-access framework could lawfully admit additional categories of registered, inspected and accountable public-transport operators.

### Options to evaluate

1. **Yellow-designated priority lanes:** assess a distinct yellow marking system for new or formally redesignated priority corridors, subject to road-marking standards, signage, visibility and consistency with existing traffic rules. Do not simply repaint current BRT lanes or use yellow markings that could be confused with existing road rules without technical approval.
2. **Operator accreditation:** establish transparent eligibility and audit criteria, potentially including current operating licences, driver identity checks, roadworthiness, insurance, vehicle inspections, service standards, passenger complaint handling and auditable trip data.
3. **Vehicle access rules:** specify which services may use which corridor, during what hours, on what stretches, and under what conditions. Consider scheduled buses and franchised operators first; assess taxis and e-hailing vehicles only if the network's capacity, safety and public transport objectives support inclusion.
4. **Physical separation:** commission a road-safety and traffic-engineering assessment before installing barriers. Trial removable delineators or other approved devices only at suitable stretches. Preserve emergency access, pedestrian crossings, drainage, bus stops, junction turning movements, safe merging and access for authorised road works. Continuous concrete barriers may be costly and may create new risks if poorly designed.
5. **Enforcement and public education:** pair signage and public awareness with consistent, lawful enforcement, fair appeal procedures and anti-corruption safeguards. Technology-based enforcement should follow applicable privacy and evidence rules.
6. **BRT protection:** do not reduce existing BRT service reliability or passenger capacity. Establish a minimum performance threshold for existing BRT users before widening access to additional vehicles.

### Pilot design and evaluation

Select a small number of technically suitable corridors after a baseline study. Compare peak and off-peak performance before and during the trial, with a suitable comparison corridor where feasible. Collect aggregated data wherever possible and minimise personal trip data.

Measure:
- Bus and authorised vehicle travel time, variability and passenger throughput.
- BRT on-time performance, dwell time and crowding.
- General traffic flow, junction queues and delay displaced onto adjacent roads.
- Road-safety incidents, conflicts at merging points and emergency access.
- Enforcement costs, maintenance costs and cost per passenger-minute saved.
- Accessibility, pedestrian safety, effects on local businesses and feedback from operators and communities.

Publish results and independent evaluation criteria. Continue, modify or stop the pilot based on evidence; do not promise that painting a lane or installing barriers will automatically reduce congestion. Dedicated lanes can improve reliability where they are appropriately designed and enforced, but effects depend on corridor capacity, intersection design, access rules and network-wide traffic behaviour.

### Funding and public value

Request a joint feasibility study before asking the State to commit to major capital works. Compare lower-cost options—markings, signs, enforcement, signal priority and limited removable delineators—with more capital-intensive segregation. Identify capital and maintenance responsibility, procurement requirements, operating budgets and the distribution of benefits across BRT, other approved operators and the travelling public.

The public case is improved person-throughput and reliability, not a private shortcut for one company. LagosGo should not receive exclusive access unless an openly justified, lawful public procurement or concession process establishes it. Any participating service should meet the same published conditions for its class.

### Suggested wording for the government meeting

“We respectfully ask Lagos State to consider a technical feasibility study for designated public-transport priority corridors. We recognise that existing BRT lanes are governed by specific access rules; we are not asking operators to disregard them. Our proposal is to work with the responsible transport and traffic agencies to assess whether a limited, properly authorised pilot—using clear markings, transparent operator accreditation, appropriate physical separation and measurable performance targets—could improve journey reliability while protecting existing BRT users and other road users. We would welcome a costed options study and public evaluation before any permanent infrastructure or access expansion is approved.”
